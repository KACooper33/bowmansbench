## What is the difference between barebow and traditional?

Barebow and traditional are two separate competition divisions with different equipment rules. An archer who buys barebow gear and enters a traditional event will fail equipment inspection, and the reverse is also true.

The short version. Barebow permits an adjustable plunger, string walking, external weights and vibration dampeners, and a riser of any material. Traditional permits none of those. Traditional requires a wooden or laminated riser, allows only a non-adjustable arrow rest, and bans string walking. Traditional does permit string silencers, which barebow does not.

World Archery sets out barebow in Book 4 article 19.3 and traditional in article 19.4. The barebow rules are identical in Book 3 article 9.3 for target archery.

## Is instinctive a division?

No major governing body checked for this page runs a division called instinctive. Instinctive describes how an archer aims, not what they may attach to the bow.

Five class lists were checked:

- World Archery. The word appears nowhere in Book 3 or Book 4.
- The NFAA lists ten shooting styles. None is instinctive.
- The IFAA has no class close to instinctive.
- The IBO traditional rules name twelve classes. None is instinctive.
- The NFAS in the United Kingdom lists thirteen styles. None is instinctive.

World Archery did run an Instinctive division in the past, and renamed it. A 2021 World Archery bylaw still refers to "the Instinctive and Long Bow divisions". The current Book 4 lists Barebow, Traditional and Longbow instead, with an interpretation dated 22 January 2022 clarifying the Traditional Division.

Smaller and regional organisations set their own class lists, and some still use the word. Check the class list for the shoot you are entering rather than assuming this holds everywhere.

So an archer who says they shoot instinctive is describing an aiming method, without a conscious gap or string reference. They will still enter a division. Under World Archery that division is usually Traditional or Longbow. Under the NFAA it is usually Traditional.

## Can you use a plunger in traditional archery?

You cannot use an adjustable plunger in the World Archery traditional division. Article 19.4.3 permits only a non-adjustable arrow rest, and lists what is allowed: "a simple plastic self-adhesive arrow rest, a feather rest as supplied by the manufacturer, or the athlete can use the bow shelf". It then closes the door: "No other types of arrow rests are allowed."

Barebow is the opposite. Article 19.3.3 permits "an adjustable pressure button, pressure point or arrow plate", provided it offers no aid in aiming, with the pressure point placed "no further back than 2 cm towards the athlete from the pivot point of the grip".

This single rule is the most expensive difference between the two divisions. A plunger is standard barebow equipment and is not legal traditional equipment.

## Is string walking allowed in traditional archery?

String walking is not allowed in the World Archery traditional division. Article 19.4.5 states: "A single anchor or face walking is permitted. String walking is not permitted."

String walking is allowed in barebow. Article 19.3.5 states: "Face and string walking are permitted."

The NFAA takes the same position for its Traditional style: "One anchor point only is permitted."

This changes which tab an archer needs. A stringwalking tab with crawl marks suits barebow. It does not suit the traditional division, and the marks themselves are separately banned there.

## What tab can you use in each division?

Barebow permits marks on the tab. World Archery article 19.3.7 allows "marks or lines added directly to the tab or on tape placed on the face of the tab", provided they "must be uniform in size, shape and colour and may have up to two different lengths". Barebow also permits an anchor plate attached to the tab.

The traditional division permits neither. Article 19.4.7 states that "markings added by the athlete, whether or not uniform in size, shape and colour, are not permitted in the traditional division", and that an anchor plate "is not permitted". It adds that when shooting, "the index finger or middle finger will be within 3 mm of the nock or touch the nock".

## Are string silencers allowed in barebow and traditional?

String silencers are the rule that runs the opposite way to every other item on this page. Traditional allows them and barebow does not.

World Archery traditional, article 19.4.2: "String silencers are also permitted, provided they are located no closer than 30 cm from the nocking point." The longbow division carries the same permission in article 19.5.2.

World Archery barebow, article 19.3.2: "No lip or nose marks or other attachments to the string are permitted." A string silencer is an attachment to the string.

The NFAA disagrees with World Archery here. The NFAA permits string silencers in Barebow Recurve as well as Traditional: "Brush buttons and string silencers, properly placed, may be used."

## What riser can you use in the traditional division?

The World Archery traditional division requires a wooden riser. Article 19.4.1 states that "the riser is of laminated construction and includes wooden laminates or is made of one piece of wood".

The bow may be a take-down type and may carry factory-installed metal fittings for limb attachment and bushings. Limbs may be adjustable for poundage and tiller.

Barebow places no material restriction on the riser. This is why most barebow risers are aluminium or carbon, and most traditional risers are wood.

## Can you put weights on a traditional bow?

You cannot bolt weights onto a traditional bow. World Archery article 19.4.1 permits weights "inside the bow riser ... if installed during the manufacturing process and not post-construction". Those weights must be "completely invisible on the exterior of the riser".

Limb dampeners attached directly to the limbs are permitted in the traditional division.

Barebow permits external weights. The 2022 bylaw allows weights and dampeners "below and above the riser's grip", provided they do not aid aiming or ranging, and provided the whole bow passes a 12.2 cm ring "without moving, flexing or turning the attachments", under article 19.3.6.

## Where does the longbow division fit?

The longbow division is stricter than traditional on every point that costs money. World Archery article 19.5 requires a bow of traditional longbow shape, permits only the arrow shelf as a rest, and states in article 19.5.4 that "string and face walking are not permitted".

Article 19.5.5 is the clearest line: "No weights, stabilisers or vibration dampeners are allowed." Internal weights installed at manufacture are the only exception. Longbow does permit string silencers, at 30 cm from the nocking point, like traditional.

## How do the two divisions compare, item by item?

|---|---|---|
| Sight | Not allowed | Not allowed |
| Adjustable plunger | Permitted | Not permitted |
| Arrow rest | Adjustable pressure point allowed | Non-adjustable rest or the shelf only |
| String walking | Permitted | Not permitted |

## Where does the NFAA differ from World Archery?

Two differences matter when an archer shoots under both bodies.

The NFAA permits string silencers in Barebow Recurve. World Archery does not permit them in barebow. An archer who shoots NFAA field and then enters a World Archery event must remove them.

The NFAA permits a stabiliser in its Traditional style: "One straight stabilizer, coupling device included, if used, which cannot exceed 12 inches at any time as measured from the back of the bow." World Archery does not permit stabilisers in barebow, and its traditional division has no equivalent permission.

Check the rulebook of the body running the shoot. The division names match across the two bodies, but the equipment lists behind those names do not.

## Which division should you enter?

Enter barebow if you want to string walk, use a plunger, and tune with external weights. Barebow is the division with the most adjustment available to an archer shooting without a sight.

Enter traditional if you shoot a wooden riser with a single anchor and no plunger. Traditional rewards a simpler setup and bans most of what barebow archers spend money on.

Enter longbow if you shoot a longbow shape with the shelf as your rest and no weights at all.

If you describe your shooting as instinctive, that does not decide the division for you. Pick the division that matches your equipment, then check that your aiming method is allowed within it.
